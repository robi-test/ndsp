Principles

The NHS already has ten design principles, including designing for trust, being inclusive, testing assumptions and doing the hard work to make complex services simple.

Rather than repeating those, the NDSP principles make them specific to the portal.

1. Start with established NHS standards

Use NHS styles, components and patterns before creating something new. Extend them only where there is a clear NDSP user need.

2. Make complex data tasks understandable

Do not expose unnecessary technical, organisational or collection complexity to users. Explain what is happening using familiar language and clear steps.

3. Keep the user's context visible

Make it clear which collection, organisation, reporting period and submission window the user is viewing or changing.

4. Prevent mistakes and support recovery

Use constraints, validation and confirmation at the right time. When something goes wrong, explain what happened, what was affected and what the user needs to do next.

5. Make status and consequences explicit

Clearly communicate whether something is pending, processing, accepted, rejected, closed or unavailable. Do not rely on colour alone.

6. Support both frequent and occasional users

Provide efficient interactions for experienced operational users without assuming everyone remembers the process, terminology or previous decisions.

7. Standardise through evidence

Do not turn a one-off solution into a standard component until it has been reviewed, tested and shown to be reusable.
